How we recruit

Six steps from brief to first day

A disciplined process means fewer bad hires and no surprises on cost or timing.

  1. Brief & workforce plan

    We agree the roles, numbers, qualifications, pay and timeline with you, and confirm the budget up front.

  2. Sourcing

    Targeted advertising, our candidate database, community networks and, for foreign roles, partner agencies in the region.

  3. Screening & testing

    Shortlisting against your criteria, written or practical tests, and document and certificate checks.

  4. Interviews & references

    Competency-based interviews, run by us or with your panel, followed by reference checks.

  5. Offer & contracts

    Offers, employment contracts compliant with the Labour Act 2017 and, for foreign hires, work permit coordination.

  6. Onboarding & follow-up

    Induction, payroll set-up and a check-in with you and the new hire after the first weeks.

Volume & project hiring

Hundreds of workers, in weeks, across the country

National programmes, elections, censuses, surveys and large projects need big field teams fast. We have designed workforce mobilisation for programmes at national scale.

  • Phased mobilisation: supervisors first, so they can help recruit and lead the field teams.
  • Simple, fair screening: a one-page application that doubles as the screening test.
  • Clear talent pools, such as teachers, school leavers and literate retirees, matched to each role.
  • Pay scales benchmarked to the NGO Forum South Sudan Salary Survey.
  • Reporting lines and training built in before deployment.
Questions

What employers ask us

How long does recruitment take?

Most single professional roles take three to five weeks from brief to offer. Volume and project hiring is planned around your deployment date; we agree the timeline at the brief stage.

Do candidates pay any fees?

No. The employer pays for recruitment. We follow fair recruitment principles and never charge job seekers to be considered or placed.

Can you hire for us if we have no office in South Sudan?

Yes. Overseas companies can use us to recruit, contract and pay staff in South Sudan, and to manage day-to-day labour matters, until they set up their own HR function.

Do you help with work permits for foreign staff?

Yes. We prepare the documentation and coordinate the application with the relevant authorities, and we brief new arrivals on local requirements.

How is recruitment priced?

Single placements are usually a fee per hire. Volume and project hiring is quoted per programme. HR outsourcing is a monthly fee based on headcount. We confirm costs in writing before we start.

What if a new hire doesn't work out?

Tell us early. We review what went wrong with you and agree a replacement approach. Replacement terms are set out in your service agreement.
